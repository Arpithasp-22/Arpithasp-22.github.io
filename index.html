    About

    Across these projects the same instinct shows up: don't trust a system just because it sounds confident — measure it, score it, and make the reasoning visible. That habit didn't start with AI.

    My path here started in biology, not code — a bachelor's in Biotechnology, then a master's in Biomedical Genetics, working as a data science intern at Manipal Hospitals on genetic counselling data: curating and validating 500+ patient cases and contributing to a condition-prediction model that improved diagnostic accuracy by roughly 5%. That same instinct for careful, checkable evidence carried straight into a second master's in Data Science and then into an AI/ML internship at ParadigmIT, where I built production LLM systems — a RAG pipeline querying over 1,000+ document chunks, a hallucination-detection and guardrails framework, and an evaluation benchmark covering 300+ prompts for accuracy, refusal behaviour, and latency.

    The throughline has stayed consistent even as the domain changed: I treat a confident-sounding answer — whether it's a diagnostic model or an LLM — with the same skepticism a clinical dataset deserves, and I'd rather build the guardrails, evaluation harnesses, and scoring engines to prove it's right than just hope it is.

    Away from the screen: painting, cycling, gym sessions, and baking — possibly the only place where getting the measurements exactly right actually is the whole point.
